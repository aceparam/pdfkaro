## Steps to compress a PDF to 500 KB

1. Select a PDF, perhaps a report or a brochure with a handful of photos. Dragging it onto this page, or pasting it, also selects it.
2. Press “Compress to 500 KB and download” and wait while the progress line counts through the pictures.
3. When the result appears, check the new size next to the old one, then press Download.

## Questions

### What kind of file is 500 KB a good target for?

Documents that are mostly text with a few photos or charts: a CV with a portrait, a short report, a letter with a logo and a scanned signature. For these, 500 KB usually leaves the photos looking fine on screen.

### What keeps its full quality?

Everything that is not a picture. Typed text stays as text you can select and search, fonts stay the same, and lines, tables and charts drawn as shapes stay sharp at any zoom. Only the photos and scanned images inside the PDF are made smaller.

### Why is my result well under 500 KB?

The tool works in steps, from mild to strong. It writes the mildest step it expects to fit, then measures the real file. Sometimes even the mildest step shrinks the photos far more than needed, because they were much sharper than any screen can show. You then get a file comfortably under the limit.

### How are 500 KB counted?

As 500,000 bytes, not 512,000. A form that counts in either way accepts a file of that size, so the target errs on the safe side. The row that lists your file counts in 1,024s, so its size can look a little lower than the number this target uses.

### Are pictures saved without loss turned into JPGs?

Never. A screenshot or diagram stored without loss may be scaled down, but it is stored without loss again, so text inside it does not blur.

### Could anything stop it reaching 500 KB?

Large fonts, attached files, or pictures this tool leaves alone, such as CMYK photos, JPEG 2000 images or black and white scans. If the target is out of reach, the page offers the smallest file it made, in a sentence naming its size. When even that saves less than 1% of your file, no new file is offered, and the page tells you why.

### Does my document leave my computer?

No. It is compressed by your browser on your device and is not uploaded. A password on the file is kept, and a digital signature will no longer be valid.
