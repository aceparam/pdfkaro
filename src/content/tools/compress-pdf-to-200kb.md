## How to make a scanned PDF 200 KB or smaller

1. Choose your scan: a certificate, an ID card, a mark sheet or a signed form. You can drop the file onto the page or paste it instead.
2. Press the button “Compress to 200 KB and download”. There are no choices to make.
3. Once the heading says your file is now 200 KB or smaller, press Download and attach the new file to the form.

## Questions

### Why do online forms ask for 200 KB?

Many application portals cap each document at about 200 KB to keep their storage small. A phone photo of one page, or a scan straight from an office scanner, is usually several times that, because the scanner keeps far more detail than a screen needs.

### How is a scan made smaller?

A scanned page is a picture. The tool first redraws it at 150 pixels to the inch across the page, which is still sharp on screen and on paper, and saves it as a JPG at a moderate quality. If that is not enough it tries stronger steps, with fewer pixels and lower quality, and keeps the mildest step whose file fits.

### Does a colour scan behave differently from a grey one?

A colour scan has three channels, so it starts larger and gains more. A grey scan stays grey to the eye, though it may be saved as a colour JPG. A pure black and white scan, stored one bit per dot, is left alone, as are CMYK pictures.

### Is 200 KB counted as 200,000 bytes?

Yes. Portals differ: some read 200 KB as 200,000 bytes, others as 204,800. Making the file 200,000 bytes or less passes either check. So a file the page lists as 198 KB is still above the target here and can be compressed.

### What if my scan cannot reach 200 KB?

The page hands you the smallest file it made, says its size, and says it is still over 200 KB. Removing blank or extra pages, or splitting the document, usually closes the gap.

### Is the scan uploaded to a server?

No. The scan is processed on your own device, inside this browser tab. It is never sent to this site or anyone else.

### What else stays the same?

Pages, any text, links and form fields are kept, and a password stays on the file. A digital signature stops being valid, because the file is saved again.
