## How to turn JPG photos into a PDF

1. Choose your photos. You can choose several at once, drop them anywhere on this page, or paste them.
2. Put them in order with “Move up” and “Move down”, or drag them. Each photo becomes one page.
3. Press “Convert and download”.
4. When the PDF is ready, press Download.

## Questions

### Are my photos uploaded?

No. The PDF is made in your browser, on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. When you press Start again or leave the page, the page forgets the photos and the PDF.

### I took photos of a document with my phone. Will they come out the right way up?

Yes. A phone often stores a photo on its side and notes how it was held. The page reads that note and turns each page to match, so the PDF looks as the photos do in your phone's gallery.

### Will my photos lose quality?

No. A JPG goes into the PDF exactly as it was compressed, without being compressed again, so the PDF is about the size of the photos added up. If it is too large to send, compress the PDF afterwards.

### Does the PDF keep where and when the photos were taken?

No. Photos often carry the camera's name, the date and the place they were taken. None of that is copied into the PDF, and the PDF has no title, author or date of its own. Only the pictures and their colours are kept.

### What size are the pages?

Every page is A4: upright for a photo that is taller than wide, on its side for a wider one. Each photo is centred, as large as fits, with no margin. A small photo is not blown up past its own size, so it stays sharp; it sits in the middle of the page.

### Can I print it?

Yes. Because the photos reach the edges of the page, a printer that cannot print to the edge may trim a thin strip. If that matters, use your printer's “fit to page” setting.

### My phone saves photos as HEIC. Can I use them?

An iPhone turns a photo into a JPG when a page asks for one, so choosing photos from your library usually works. If a file is refused, save it as a JPG first.

### Why was my photo refused?

A file that is not a JPG or a PNG is not added. A JPG that is damaged, or saved in an unusual way that many PDF readers cannot show, stays in the list with a note saying what to do: open it in a photo app and save a copy as a JPG.
