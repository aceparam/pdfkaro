## How to turn PNG pictures into a PDF

1. Choose your PNG pictures or screenshots. You can choose several at once, or drop them anywhere on this page.
2. To add a screenshot you have just taken, paste it onto the page.
3. Put the pictures in order with “Move up” and “Move down”, or drag them. Each picture becomes one page.
4. Press “Convert and download”, then Download when the PDF is ready.

## Questions

### Are my pictures uploaded?

No. The PDF is made in your browser, on your device, and nothing is sent anywhere. When you press Start again or leave the page, the page forgets the pictures and the PDF.

### Will my screenshots stay sharp?

Yes. A PNG with 8 bits per colour, as screenshots are, is stored in the PDF without loss: every pixel is kept as it was. For 16-bit pictures, see below. A small screenshot is not stretched to fill the page, so text in it stays crisp. It is placed in the middle of an A4 page at its own size.

### What happens to see-through parts?

They show the white of the page. A logo or a cut-out with a see-through background comes out on white.

### Why is the PDF bigger than my PNG files?

A PNG can store a picture with few colours, such as a screenshot of a page, in a very compact way. In the PDF each pixel is stored in full colour, which can make a page larger than the PNG it came from, sometimes about three times as large. If the PDF is too big to send, compress it afterwards.

### Can I use an animated PNG?

Yes, but a PDF page cannot move, so only the first picture of the animation is used.

### My PNG has 16 bits per colour. Is anything lost?

A little. The PDF stores 8 bits per colour, which is what screens show, so the finest steps between shades in a 16-bit picture are rounded. You will not usually see the difference.

### Is the text in a screenshot searchable in the PDF?

No. Each page is a picture of the screen. You can read it, but you cannot select or search its words.

### Is there a limit on size?

There is no fixed limit, as the work happens on your device. A picture of very many pixels can be slow, and a phone may run out of memory. The page warns you about a picture of more than 50 million pixels, or files that add up to more than 100 MB.
